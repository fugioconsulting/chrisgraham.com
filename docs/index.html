I was shattered, but then I found a path forward: just do the next scary thing.

More About My Story

My first business was being a singer-songwriter. Touring, selling records, playing with bands. Then I became a music producer and audio mastering engineer.

About ten years in, after reading a few stacks of business books and working with thousands of independent artists and major labels like Sony, Warner, and Disney, a friend and I launched the first business podcast for the recording industry.

When it went viral, people started asking for business coaching. Before I knew it, I was helping Emmy, Grammy, Oscar, and Tony winners grow their businesses and themselves. I felt like I was finally one of the cool kids.

In 2020, things got exceptionally weird.

Covid shut everything down, and my life and mental health fell apart. Luckily, right before my breakdown, I had built the first agentic AI in the audio industry. "Bounce Butler" was a hit. Recording studios around the world still use him every day. The income my little AI brought in was just enough to keep the wheels on while I hit rock bottom, was hospitalized, diagnosed with PTSD, and started EMDR therapy.

EMDR was the hardest thing I have ever done. I finally met myself, and on the other side of some healing, I found the recipe for post-traumatic growth: just do the next scary thing.

One of the most meaningful moments of my journey was sitting beside my sons in the Ohio Senate before a unanimous vote to pass The Scout's Honor Law. A few senators stood and spoke about a priest survivor who fought for Ohio's scout survivors and won. My sons watched as their dad's crazy idea clawed back justice for 1,911 survivors in Ohio.

Then we started hearing from across the country. Other states were passing The Scout's Honor Law too. Alabama, Indiana, and Iowa quickly followed Ohio's lead. 5,000+ survivors now had a path to justice. Hundreds of millions of dollars in settlements.

Today I'm building movements for employee owners, survivors, and award-winning creatives. Home is a log cabin in the woods near Granville, Ohio. I've been part of building movements that changed the world, but the best thing I've ever done is show my kids that no matter how dark your path is, life is always full of possibilities.

Just do the next scary thing.

The Fugio Framework

Ben Franklin mastered accomplishing the impossible. In 1787 he designed the United States' first coin: the Fugio cent.

Fugio, 1787, Mind Your Business.

Fugio is Latin for "I fly." The sun and the sundial are time, and time is speaking: I fly. Beneath it, the only advice the fledgling federal government ever stamped on its money: mind your business.

"Mind your business" is an insult with a secret. It does not say you are less. It says you are being your own worst enemy right now. What Ben Franklin meant was: identify your mission, then figure out how to complete it as efficiently as possible. Mind your business means be your own best friend.

"A house divided against itself cannot stand." Abraham Lincoln said it of a nation in 1858. It is just as true of a person, a team or a business. When we are our own worst enemy, we are the house divided.

What does it mean to mind your business today?

Whether our business is a company, an office, a campaign, or a duty in the service of others, it comes down to three questions. Together they are our AI strategy:

  1. What is the mission? Who are we serving, and what are we trying to accomplish?
  2. What should we use AI for? How do we help our people major in their majors and minor in their minors?
  3. What should we never use AI for? Which parts of the mission are the most human, and how do we make them more human?

Lock those three down and we fly. I help mission-driven people and teams do it three ways.

I teach you how to fly. We'll use the same Fugio Framework I've used with Emmy, Grammy, Oscar and Tony winners, legislators, journalists and business leaders to help you work smarter, not harder, using cutting-edge AI.

ART is the key

Before EMDR therapy, I had a lot to process. So I made art. I took photos. I played guitar. I worked in recording studios. I stayed up all night building custom AI's. I kept my mind busy until eventually, I broke.
​
​On the other side of being hospitalized, going to EMDR therapy,  hundreds of yoga classes, thousands of hours of meditation, and a whole lot of art therapy, something magical happened... I filed a police report, and they found a witness... but the case was then closed because Ohio has some of the worst laws and some of the shortest "Statute of Limitation" in the developed world for survivors of our worst crimes.

I started showing up at the Ohio Statehouse. Loitering in the lobby during votes. Making friends with legislators. Showing them the custom AI's I've built that are smarter than any statehouse lobbyist.

​I started brining my camera to the Statehouse. I'd take a black and white photo of a legislator in action, make a large photo print on museum quality paper, then go back to the Statehouse and give the legislator my art.

Rep Jessica Miranda. The "bad ass suburban mom" & my bestie. We've got very different perspectives, and an amazing friendship!

One day, Coleen said something to me that gave me goosebumps: "Chris, you're a Citizen Lobbyist. You should teach people how you do it."

So I went to work. I brought film makers to the Statehouse. I brought musicians to the Statehouse. 
I brought survivors, artists, activists & advocates to the Statehouse.

I taught them how to meet and collaborate with law makers. They started getting their own legislation rolling.

As I'd show a legilsator their photo, I'd usually crack a few dad-jokes and give them a bit of coaching;

"I think it's time to redefine your legacy."
"Let's make this justice reform law together."
​"You were made for this."

I was doing advocacy with art, and it was working!
I started running all over the Statehouse to manage all the bills I had in motion. I had become a new kind of Citizen Lobbyist.
